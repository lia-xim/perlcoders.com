About · history · disclosure

An independent publication, relaunched in 2026.

PerlCoders is a living English-language technical publication and tool lab about practical Perl, programming-language decisions, web automation and the history of the programmable web.

Plainly

Five things this publication is not.

  1. 01Not the former PerlCoders companyThe domain changed hands. We did not acquire the company, its software, its customers, its contracts or its obligations, and we do not claim its credits or its clients. The Archive exists so that inbound links from the old catalogue land somewhere honest instead of on a 404 or a sales page.
  2. 02Not an official Perl organisationNo affiliation with the Perl core team, The Perl and Raku Foundation, CPAN or any related body. No official Perl branding, logo or mascot is used anywhere on this site.
  3. 03Not a museumHistory is roughly a third of what we do and it is treated as an asset, not as the product. The Timeline’s last node is deliberately open.
  4. 04Not an advocacy siteComparisons here reach conclusions against Perl regularly, and publish them with the same prominence as the ones that go the other way. If a section ever reads as promotion, that is a bug.
  5. 05Not a funnel for another productThere is one disclosed commercial relationship, described in full below. It does not appear above the fold on the homepage, in the navigation, or on history pages.

How verification works

Every factual record on this site carries a verification state, and the templates are forbidden from rendering an unverified claim as though it were confirmed. That is a schema rule enforced by js/validate-content.js, not an editorial intention.

Verified
An editor checked the claim against a primary source. The source is listed on the page and the check has a date.
Unverified
Plausible and widely repeated, but not confirmed against a primary source by us. Shown, marked, and open for correction.
Typed slot
A field that exists in the model with no value in it. It renders as an empty slot with the source an editor must check — never as a zero, a dash or an estimate.

Six of seventeen Timeline events are currently unverified. Three of four items on the homepage evidence strip are empty typed slots. Both numbers are visible on the pages themselves.

Relationship disclosure

Commercial relationship

PerlCoders shares ownership with Contextter, a commercial product in search engineering and crawler analysis. It is a related product, not the reason this publication exists.

What that means in practice:

  • Contextter is named on this page, and on any research report where it was used as a working tool. Those reports carry a disclosure block in the body, not a footnote.
  • There is no sitewide keyword link, no homepage banner, no navigation entry and no call to action on history or archive pages.
  • No comparison, guide or lab recommends it, and none of the labs on this site send data anywhere.
  • Outbound links carry UTM parameters so the referral is measurable — and therefore auditable — rather than hidden.

If you find this relationship influencing editorial judgement anywhere on the site, that is a correction we want to receive.

Funding This build carries no advertising, no sponsored content, no affiliate links and no tracking beyond what the host serves. If that changes, this section changes first and the change is announced in Pulse.

The name

Why keep it at all?

Because the alternative is worse for the people still following old links.

The honest argument against reusing the name is that it invites confusion with a company that no longer exists. The argument for it is that thousands of inbound links, archived indexes and twenty-year-old bookmarks point at these URLs, and the choice is between a publication that explains what happened and a parked page that does not.

We resolve it by being explicit rather than quiet: this page, the footer of every page, and the status banner on every Archive entry all say the same thing in the same words.
